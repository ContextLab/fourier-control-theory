Fourier Series ↔ Robot Arms

A chain of spinning links is a complex Fourier series in disguise. Drag the arm, drag the spectrum, draw your own shape — watch the two views of the same math move together.

The puppet arm: each link is one Fourier component — length = amplitude, spin rate = frequency, starting angle = phase. The fading trail is the end-effector path z(t). With the human-arm preset, the bones (upper arm, forearm, hand, and finger joints) are the components and the skin deforms around them. Drag a joint to rotate that bone (everything downstream follows); shift-drag to also change the bone's length.

Power/amplitude spectrum: one stem per frequency. Drag a stem up/down for amplitude, left/right to change frequency, or around its head to change phase.

Tracking error—

Truncating to |f| ≤ B is a hard low-pass filter on actuator bandwidth: only components inside the passband survive, so the tip can only track band-limited paths. The lag toggle instead applies a smooth first-order filter H(f) = 1/(1 + i f/fc), shrinking and phase-delaying every component — a more realistic actuator model.
